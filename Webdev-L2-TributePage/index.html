ABOUT HIM

A Life Dedicated to India

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist, author and educator who became one of the country's most admired public figures. His journey from a modest background to the highest constitutional office in India became an inspiration for millions of young people.

His scientific career was closely connected with India's space and defence programmes. He contributed to important national projects and played a significant role in the development of India's launch vehicle and missile programmes.

In 2002, he became the 11th President of India. During his presidency, he was widely admired for his approachable personality and his strong connection with students and young people.

Beyond science and public service, Kalam encouraged young people to dream, learn and contribute to the development of the nation. His books, speeches and interactions with students continue to inspire people across generations.

HIS JOURNEY

Major Milestones

1931

Early Life

Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam grew up in a modest family and developed an early interest in learning and science.

1960s

Space Research

He joined India's space research efforts and became involved in important launch vehicle development programmes.

1980

Satellite Launch Vehicle

The successful launch of the Rohini satellite by India's SLV-III programme marked an important milestone in the country's space programme.

2002

President of India

Kalam became the 11th President of India and served in the office from 2002 to 2007.

2015

Enduring Legacy

Kalam passed away while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong, leaving behind a lasting legacy of education and inspiration.
